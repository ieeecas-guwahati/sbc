In-Memory Computing on Memristor Crossbars

Inaugural eventInvited online lecture
Date
26 September 2026 (Saturday)
Time
11:00 AM – 12:00 PM
Venue
EEE Seminar Room, 2nd Floor, Department of EEE, IIT Guwahati

The inaugural event of the IEEE Circuits and Systems Society Student Branch Chapter, IIT Guwahati, held jointly with the IEEE CASS Kolkata Section Chapter (CAS04-Guwahati, CH11218). Faculty members, research scholars and students attended the lecture in the EEE Seminar Room, with the speaker joining online.

Talk overview

Resistive memory technologies are very promising both for designing non-volatile resistive memories and for providing in-memory computation. This could help bridge the processor–memory bottleneck, which is crucial in conventional von Neumann architectures.

Memristors are one technology that can be used to build such resistive systems. In addition to a very low silicon footprint and reduced power consumption, memristor crossbars can carry out various kinds of computation in the memory fabric itself. The talk covered several logic design styles proposed for this, including material implication logic (IMPLY), memristor-aided logic (MAGIC) and majority function logic. Memristor crossbars can also be used as neuromorphic accelerators, where vector–matrix multiplication can be carried out in a single step.

Invited speaker

Prof. Indranil Sengupta

Emeritus Professor, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur

Prof. Sengupta joined IIT Kharagpur in 1988 and holds a PhD from the University of Calcutta. His research spans VLSI design and testing, reversible and quantum computing, and cryptography and information security, including memristor-based logic design and in-memory computing. He has published extensively in leading IEEE and ACM journals and conferences and has served as Vice-Chancellor of JIS University.
